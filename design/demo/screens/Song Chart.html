Your hands, your pace.

No auto-scroll. Two large page buttons move the chart a screen at a time, so long intros and solos don't throw you off. The chart is now its own view, and the song view no longer has a preview. The second half of the page answers how you enter a chart. Phones 2 and 5 work.

Paging, not scrolling

Next is the big orange button at the thumb, and Back is smaller beside it (Fitts's Law). Each tap moves about one screen with a short slide, and the last two lines stay at the top so you keep your place (Working Memory). Bluetooth pedals and the top/bottom halves of the screen do the same thing.

Chart as its own view

Someone either needs the chords or they don't, so the song view holds only a Chords & lyrics button. The song view keeps its space for facts, links and every note layer. Songs without a chart show Add chords & lyrics in the same spot (Hick's Law).

Chords pinned to letters

Behind the scenes, a chart is lyrics plus chords attached to character positions (ChordPro). Lyrics and chords are edited separately. Change the words and each chord moves with the letter it's attached to, so nothing drifts out of line (Tesler's Law).

Three ways in

Paste chords-over-lyrics from a tab site and it's converted. Type the lyrics, then tap chords onto the words, using a palette built from the song's key. Copy chords from verse 1 to the next verses in one tap (Pareto: most songs repeat).
