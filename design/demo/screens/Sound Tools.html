Rock On · Source · Sound tools

Find the note.

Three small sound tools, all with real audio, so turn your sound on. Tap any chord in a chart, or open the Chords viewer above it, to hear it strummed. A starting note gives a cappella openings a pitch to sing from. A tuner already knows the song's tuning. The audio is generated in the app (a plucked-string model), so it works offline and needs no sample files.

Tap a chord, hear it

One Chords button in the controls row opens a centred chord viewer: the song's chords in order of first use (transposed with the key), a diagram and fingering, ‹ › and chips to switch, and Play. Tapping a chord in the text opens the viewer on that chord. Every target in the viewer is 40px or more. Sound starts within 30ms (Doherty Threshold).

Starting note

A speaker button at the end of the chart controls: 🔈 D. Tap to hear it, hold to change it. Tap it to hear the note to sing from. In the editor, two buttons play the key chord and the note separately, no settings needed. It's set once per chart, on a one-octave piano with large keys, and shown relative to the key so it still makes sense after transposing (Tesler's Law).

Tuner that knows the song

The tuner opens on the song's tuning and highlights the strings that changed from the last one. It picks up which string you're playing, and "4 of 6 in tune" counts down as you go (Goal-Gradient). Pedal-style feedback: the letter, needle and arrows run from red at ±50 cents to green in tune. Chevrons on the side you're off point toward the note, three when far out, two when closer, one when nearly there, and one on each side when you're in tune (Goal-Gradient, Jakob's Law: it reads like a tuner pedal).

Where they live

The chord viewer and starting note are in the chart. The tuner is under More → Tuner, and also behind every retune line and the practice-session tuning alert, one tap from where you notice you need it (Flow). None of this is in the spec, so I've logged it as new.
